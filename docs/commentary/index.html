Prediction, lived experience and whose goals a model serves.

Knowing People Without Making Them

A Theory of Closure, Experience, and Person-Aligned Prediction in Complex Social Systems

Does a model understand people — or make them easier to predict? This paper distinguishes predictability discovered through information from predictability manufactured by shaping someone’s environment. It connects that distinction to lived experience, the exclusion of data-poor groups, and prediction directed toward a person’s own reflectively endorsed goals.

  • World closureHow much uncertainty is resolved by variables a predictor controls.
  • The human residualExperience can explain differences that demographic models leave behind.
  • Data captureLess recorded data can mean less recognition of genuine need.
  • Person-aligned predictionSupport the person’s goals, with self-authored limits on steering.

The paper makes three claims about prediction in social systems. Each simulation below is a small, controlled model built to make one claim visible: accuracy can be manufactured, missing data can hide need, and experience explains what demographics miss.

Illustrative simulations under stated assumptions, not empirical evidence about real people. The paper sets out nine falsifiable hypotheses for future testing.
